# The community loop

What the club says turns into proposals. Proposals pass a safety review. A safe change ships behind a flag to a canary
of beta members, and turns itself off when it does harm. Code makes every decision; models only write short sentences
that code then checks. Code, not a model, deploys, and only a C0 change (a declared setting, inside its range) ships
without a person.

The numbers below come from a simulated club. **Simulated club: members and requests generated by code (seed 7). The
pipeline, gates and rollback are real.**

How the parts fit, and who built what: [community_loop_contract.md](community_loop_contract.md). The safety review in
full: [community_loop_review.md](community_loop_review.md) and [never.md](never.md). Shipping and rollback in full:
[community_loop_shipping.md](community_loop_shipping.md).

## The stages

| # | Stage | Code | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Signals | `agent/src/loop/signals.js` | With `/improve on`, a member's agent counts 👍 and 👎 per feature, requests it could not meet, and answers to "want a game?". It keeps no words. |
| 2 | One item, one yes | `signals.offerShare`, `share` | When the agent could not help (or with `/idea`), Muse Glimmer writes the request as one short sentence. Code checks it, and the member taps Share or Don't. |
| 3 | Screen | `loop/review.js` `screen` (part B) | Prompt Guard 2 and Llama Guard 3 on the CPU, and code's checks again. A required guard that is down holds the item until it answers. |
| 4 | Ideas | `agent/src/loop/proposals.js` `cluster`, `rank`, `vote` | DRAMA groups the shared sentences, and Muse Glimmer names groups that ask for the same change. An idea shows with 5 distinct members. It becomes a proposal only if it bridges: support that one side of the club does not explain (Community Notes' factorization), plus weekday and weekend support. Members vote quadratically, and each side's votes are matched. |
| 5 | Spec | `proposals.summarize`, `draft` | Muse Glimmer summarizes the idea from its sentences, then drafts a spec from that summary alone. Code writes the counts, the oracle for a setting, the privacy line and the Rule-of-Two rows. |
| 6 | Review | `review.check` (part B) | The never-list, the class (C0 to C3, from what the change does), and the support recounted from the items and votes. |
| 7 | Gates | `loop/gates.js` (part C) | Policy, code scan, unit tests, the crash matrix, the red team, Messenger end to end, the Autopilot at pass^3 = 1.0, the oracle, the privacy canary. |
| 8 | Ship | `loop/flags.js` `ship` (part C) | A C0 change goes to the canary: the beta members in an HMAC bucket under 20%, one canary at a time, for 7 days. |
| 9 | Watch | `flags.monitor`, `loop/cycle.js` `watch` | A hard breach (a booking without a yes, a double booking, the privacy marker leaving, the guard flagging more of what models write) turns the flag off at once. A soft one turns it off and reopens the proposal: 👎 up 10 points and beyond chance (a one-sided z of 1.645), or the requests not falling. A clean canary is promoted to everyone, and the club gets a note made by code. |

`loop/cycle.js` runs stages 3 to 9 as one turn: nightly in the gateway with `LOOP_NIGHTLY=1`, and the monitor every
five minutes. A C1, C2 or C3 change stops at stage 6 and waits for the maintainer (`POST /api/loop/decide`). After
their yes the builder (part D) works on a branch, the gates run, and the maintainer merges.

## Consent and privacy

- **Two tiers.** `/improve on` lets the agent count, with no words. It counts:
  - 👍 and 👎 per feature;
  - unmet requests by source: a Llama Guard refusal, the "can't answer" fallback, or a request no tool covers;
  - answers to "want a game?".

  `/improve off` stops it and deletes the counts. Words reach the club only one item at a time: "Share with the club
  as “…”? Nobody sees who said it." [Share] [Don't]. `/idea` is its own request to share, so it needs no `/improve on`.
- **Who.** Loop data never holds a player id. Every row is keyed by `who`, an HMAC-SHA256 of the id with a server
  secret (`LOOP_SECRET`, or `agent/data/loop/secret`, made once with mode 600 and never committed).
- **Words stay home until shared.** The words of an unmet request are kept in the player's own record (`store.js`),
  never in loop data. A Llama Guard refusal is counted, and its words are never offered for sharing.
- **What code checks in a sentence** (`signals.checkText`, then `review.screen` again):
  - 120 characters at most;
  - plain English;
  - no name of a member or a venue, and no capitalized word that could be a name or a place;
  - no number;
  - no link, address or handle;
  - no body or health term.

  Glimmer gets one more try with the problems listed. After that the sentence is refused and nothing is offered.
- **Body and health never become signals.** A thumb on the body log, the report or the health import is not counted.
  An unmet request about the body or health is not counted, not even as a number. Raw words about the body are never
  sent for a paraphrase.
- **Limits.** One offer waits at a time, at most two offers a day for unmet requests, and three shared items a day.
- **/forget** deletes all of it, by recomputing the player's `who`: consent, counts, waiting offers, held and shared
  items, votes, and the player's place in every beta list (`flags.forget`). `test/e2e.mjs` checks this end to end.

## Ideas: clusters, support and votes

- **Clusters.** DRAMA (Meta's `facebook/drama-base`, on the CPU) embeds each shared sentence, and grouping happens
  only within the same part of the app (the paraphrase's `area`).
  - **Average linkage at 0.85.** Every turn, all requests are grouped again: two groups are one while their requests
    average cosine 0.85 or more. This doesn't depend on the order requests came in. A group keeps the id and the
    proposal of the old cluster it shares most items with.
  - **Then the model's merge.** Muse Glimmer, as the summarizer (no tools, community text only), reads a few requests
    from each group in one part of the app and names the groups that ask for the same change. Code merges a pair only
    when their centres are at 0.70 or more, so the model can join neighbours but never two ideas far apart.
  - The centre counts every member once, however many items they shared, so one account can't pull it.
  - A new cluster whose items average 0.85 or more to an open proposal's items is that idea again, and joins it.
  - An idea at 0.85 to something Serve already does (`agent/src/loop/features.json`, a sentence and a few short
    requests per feature) gets an answer with how to use the feature, not a proposal.
  - Each paraphrase also names its change in a few checked words (`topic`). Embedding the topic instead of the
    sentence was measured and did no better (below), so the sentence is the default (`LOOP_EMBED_ON`).
- **Shown.** An idea shows with 5 or more distinct members.
- **Bridged.** In the style of Community Notes, each rating pairs a member with an idea: 1 when they shared a request in
  it or voted for it, 0 when they took part but not in this one. The ratings are fit as r ≈ μ + b_member + b_idea +
  f_member · f_idea (1 dimension; the intercepts held back more than the factors, λ 0.15 and 0.03, as in Community
  Notes). The factor soaks up support that comes from one side: members who act alike across ideas, a faction, a ring
  of accounts.
  - An idea whose factor is 0.5 or more in size has its support explained by one side. It waits, with the reason, as
    Community Notes' own rule holds a note back.
  - With fewer than 20 members taking part there is too little to tell sides apart, and bridging is not applied.
  - The weekday-and-weekend rule stays as a second gate.
- **Score.** Each distinct member counts 2 for an unmet request or 1 for a wish, plus the quadratic votes. Votes are
  matched by side (connection-oriented cluster match, after Miller, Weyl and Erichsen): the members on one side of the
  factor (|f| ≥ 0.3) count as one voter spending all their credits, √(Σ votes²). Members on neither side count alone.
- **Votes.** 9 credits a month; n votes on one idea cost n² (`/ideas` has a vote button, `qv:<proposal>:1`, and
  `POST /api/loop/vote` sets a member's total for one idea). The review recounts every vote against each member's
  budget.

## Specs

- **The summarizer** reads the cluster's shared sentences: community text only. It has no tools and nothing private,
  and it returns a JSON Schema: one sentence of need, which code checks like a paraphrase, plus the area, when they
  play and the kind of change (enums).
- **The drafter** reads that summary, the counts, the declared settings (`flags.schema.json`) and the list of source
  files. The list shows which files are on the never-list, and why. It never reads the members' words. It returns a
  title, a problem, the change and an oracle in a JSON Schema, and code checks every sentence of it (once more with
  the problems listed, then the idea waits).
- **The never-list, before the review.** Code checks the draft's files against the never-list (`test/policy.mjs`
  `neverPath`). A file on it is taken out of the change and named. The change becomes C3, for the maintainer, with the
  reason ("it needs agent/src/play.js, which is on the never-list (booking): the builder may not change it, so a
  maintainer decides whether to make it by hand, outside the loop"). Before, the review refused such a draft outright.
- **Code writes the rest:**
  - the counts;
  - the class, from `review.classify` (never the draft's claim);
  - the oracle for a setting ("`flags.get(key, a canary member)` returns the value; everyone else gets the default");
  - the privacy line;
  - the Rule-of-Two rows, from the review's own table.
- **What a builder may read** is `spec.build`: the class, area, kind, files, key and value, plus the maintainer's own
  note from the decide route. It never gets the members' words or the drafts' sentences.

## The Agents Rule of Two, stage by stage

Meta's *Agents Rule of Two*: within one stage, at most two of untrusted input [A], private data [B] and the power to
change things [C]. The table is `RULE_OF_TWO` in `loop/review.js`, which every spec carries and the review checks.

| Stage | Where | [A] untrusted | [B] private | [C] power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| record | `signals.record` | yes: the player's own words | yes: their own record | no |
| paraphrase | `signals.offerShare` (Muse Glimmer, no tools) | yes | yes: one member's message | no: it can only offer a sentence |
| share | `signals.share` (the member's yes) | yes | no | no |
| screen | `review.screen` | yes | no | no |
| cluster | `proposals.cluster`, `vote`, `rank` | yes | no: `who` is an HMAC | no |
| summarize | `proposals.summarize`, `draft` (Muse Glimmer, no tools) | yes | no | no |
| review | `review.check`, `test/policy.mjs` | yes | no | no: a verdict |
| decide | `POST /api/loop/decide` (a person) | yes | no | yes |
| build | `builder.build` (part D) | no: the spec's fields, the note, the repository | no: no `data/`, `.env` or network | yes: a patch on a branch, never merged |
| gates | `gates.runGates` | yes: a model-written diff | no: test data and a canary | no: pass or fail |
| ship | `flags.ship` (C0 only) | yes: a value reduced to a declared type and range | no | yes |
| monitor | `flags.monitor`, `cycle.watch` | no: code's own counts | yes | yes: it can only turn a flag off |

No stage holds all three. The coding agent never reads community text. A person stands between the summaries and any
code.

## The gates

The contract's nine, in order, stopping at the first that fails ([community_loop_shipping.md](community_loop_shipping.md#the-gates)):
1. `test/policy.mjs` on the diff (for C0: `review.classify` must find a declared key inside its range);
2. a code scan of the added lines;
3. the unit tests (`unit.mjs`, `loop_unit.mjs`, `loop_ship_unit.mjs`);
4. `crash_matrix.mjs`;
5. `redteam.mjs`;
6. `e2e.mjs`;
7. `autopilot_eval.mjs` at pass^3 = 1.0;
8. the spec's oracle;
9. the privacy canary.

Gates 4 to 7 need Muse Glimmer: under `flock /tmp/serve-gpu.lock` on the server's GPU, at background priority. Each
suite must stay at or above its baseline, Autopilot tokens may rise at most 15% and p95 latency at most 20%. In
`loop_eval` a C0 change runs gates 1, 2, 3, 8 and 9 (`LOOP_SHIP_GATES`). The GPU suites are the maintainer's to run, or
the main session's, with `node test/loop_gates.mjs`.

**The earlier runs, as measured.** Each run fixed something the one before showed:
- **Run 2** (the 10-point rule alone, a fresh secret each run): the same "ask later" C0 change went to 5 canary members.
  None of them had asked for it. Their 👎 went from 29% to 52% by chance, and it was rolled back. The planted change
  rolled back too (62% → 93%).
- **Run 3** (z-test added, the secret seeded): "ask later" mixed with "pick my partner" in one cluster, and was drafted
  as C2 to wait for the maintainer. The weekend-mornings cluster shipped `ask.ahead` 3 → 7, which was promoted. The
  planted change went to `ask.hour` = 9, which the simulation gave no harm, so it was promoted. Both were fixed for
  the final run: the simulation now models harm on either key, and an item joins at 0.80 only when it is about the
  same aspect.

**Is the canary big enough?** Run 2 led to this check of the soft-breach rule on its own. It uses no model: 20,000
trials per size. Each member answers once a day, 7 days before and 7 after, and says "Not this week" 25% of the time
with no change, or 60% after a bad one:

| Canary members | A change that does nothing, rolled back: 10 points alone | With the z-test as well (now in `flags.js`) | A bad change caught, 10 points alone | With the z-test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 25.8% | 5.5% | 97.5% | 81.4% |
| 5 | 19.9% | 4.9% | 99.4% | 94.8% |
| 10 | 11.6% | 5.1% | 100% | 99.9% |
| 20 | 4.5% | 4.5% | 100% | 100% |
| 40 | 0.8% | 0.8% | 100% | 100% |

With the canary at 20% of the beta members, a club needs about 100 of them before a canary passes 20 and the rule
stops costing catches. Below that, a false rollback is cheap: one flag goes back to its default and the proposal opens
again.

### Grouping, drafts and bridging, measured

These three were measured outside `loop_eval`, over three seeds (7, 11 and 13), so the result does not rest on one
club. Simulated club: members and requests generated by code. The paraphrases, embeddings, grouping, drafts and review
are real.

**Grouping** (`test/loop_cluster_eval.mjs`, `data/loop_cluster_eval.json` and `data/loop_cluster_merge.json`). Each
seed's requests were paraphrased once by Muse Glimmer (70, 71 and 71 requests; the hostile ideas, which the screen
stops, left out). Every way of grouping was then scored against the idea each request came from. That idea is known
only to the simulation. 7 ideas per seed have 5 or more members, so 21 could surface:

| Way | Ideas surfaced (of 21) | Groups of 5+ members that mix ideas | Homogeneity | Completeness | V-measure | ARI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First version: greedy on the sentence (0.80 same aspect, 0.85 otherwise) | 14 | 2 | 0.931 | 0.757 | 0.835 | 0.620 |
| Greedy on the topic | 13 | 3 | 0.941 | 0.745 | 0.832 | 0.607 |
| Average linkage on the topic, 0.82 | 13 | 0 | 0.978 | 0.709 | 0.822 | 0.522 |
| Average linkage on the sentence, 0.85 | 13 | 0 | 0.989 | 0.702 | 0.820 | 0.530 |
| **Average linkage on the sentence, 0.85, then the model's merge (0.70)**, now the default | **14** | **0** | **0.989** | 0.741 | **0.847** | 0.619 |

- **What improved.** The new default surfaces as many ideas as the first version, and no group of 5 or more members
  mixes two ideas (the first version had two; a mixed group is how run 3 drafted the wrong change). V-measure rose from
  0.835 to 0.847.
- **What did not.** Completeness is a little lower (0.757 → 0.741), so ideas still split. "Look further ahead" split
  on every seed, in every way tried, and "pick my partner" on two seeds of three. Its requests ("look further ahead",
  "suggest games for the whole week", "tell me about Saturday games earlier") read as different changes to DRAMA and to
  the model alike.
- **The topic** (a canonical rewrite in a few checked words) did no better than the sentence, so it is kept but not
  embedded by default.
- **The model's merge** took 73 calls over the three seeds (p50 30 s on the A100).

**Drafts and the never-list** (the same eval, step 3). The drafter ran three times for each idea that surfaced, as it
was and as it is now:

| | Drafts | Refused by the review | Refused for a never-list file | Sent to the maintainer as C3, naming the file |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before: no never-list in the prompt, no check | 48 | 9 (19%) | 9 | 0 |
| After: the never-list shown, the files checked by code | 47 | **0** | 0 | 9 |

All 9 were "split the court fee", whose drafts list `agent/src/play.js` (booking). Now its spec waits for the
maintainer with the reason, instead of being refused.

**Bridging** (`test/loop_bridge_eval.mjs`, `data/loop_bridge_eval.json`, no model). Each seed's club gets a faction:
12 members, 6 weekday and 6 weekend players, so the old two-group rule lets them through. They all back two pet ideas
and put all their votes on them. The other members vote as in `loop_eval`.

| Seeds 7, 11 and 13 together | Faction ideas through (of 6) | Broad ideas through, of the 17 the old rule let through | Top of the ranking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old: 5 members, weekday and weekend, raw votes | 6 | 17 | a faction idea, on all three seeds |
| The factor alone (Community Notes' \|f\| ≥ 0.5) | 0 | 16 | "ask later" |
| **The factor, and its backers acting together elsewhere (≥ 50%)**, now the default | **0** | **17** | "ask later", on all three seeds |

- The faction's 20 to 25 raw votes per idea count 6 to 10 after matching, because one side counts as one voter.
- On `loop_eval`'s own club, with no faction, the factor alone held back an idea that nobody was pushing. The idea had
  |f| = 1.07, but only 17% of its backers backed any other same idea. The rule now in use holds back none of that club's
  ideas.

## What is real and what is simulated

| Real | Simulated |
|---|---|
| The code of every stage: consent, the HMAC, the paraphrase and its checks, the guards (Llama Guard 3 and Prompt Guard 2 on the server's CPU), DRAMA clustering, the review, the gates, the flags, the canary, the monitor, the rollback and /forget | The members: 60 from `population.js` (seed 7), and who has `/improve on` or `/beta on` |
| Muse Glimmer writing every paraphrase, summary and draft | What they say: twelve ideas, each in a few phrasings written by the developer, three of them hostile, plus a sock puppet and two poisoned items |
| The counts, votes and windows the monitor compares | How members answer "want a game?": the rates are code (for example, a member who asked for an evening question says "Not this week" 45% of the time when asked at noon and 15% when asked after work). | The time windows the code compares | The clock: a simulated September, passed to every function as `now` |

The simulation shows that the pipeline does what it was built to do with these inputs. It does not show how real
members would talk, vote or react.

## What is not built

- **Automatic merge or deploy of code.** A C1 to C3 change is built on a branch and gated, and a person merges it.
  Only a C0 setting ships by code.
- **A coding agent that reads community text.** The builder reads the spec's fields and the maintainer's note.
- **Fine-tuning on feedback.** No model is trained on what members say or tap. The loop changes settings, and through
  a person, code.
- **Pooling data across clubs.** Every club's signals stay on its own server.
- Also not here: bridging in more than one dimension, sock-puppet defence beyond one HMAC per Messenger account and a
  side found by the factorization, and a nightly turn tested over real weeks.

## Prior work

These are in the documentation only, never in the app:
- **Darwin Gödel Machine** (arXiv 2505.22954): a self-improving coding agent that hacked its own evaluation, which is
  why the tests are read-only to the builder here.
- **AlphaEvolve** (arXiv 2506.13131): changes proposed by a model and kept only when automated evaluators pass them,
  as the oracle and the gates do here.
- **ReasoningBank** (arXiv 2509.25140): agents learning from experience distilled into short strategies, here the
  members' experience reduced to checked sentences.
- **ACE**, Agentic Context Engineering (arXiv 2510.04618): playbooks that evolve by small, itemized updates. Here that
  is the versioned, one-key-at-a-time history taken over from `evolve.js`.
- **Misevolution** (arXiv 2509.26354): self-evolving agents can drift into unsafe behaviour. This is why every change
  passes the same safety review and comes with an automatic rollback.
- **Clio** (arXiv 2412.13678): privacy-preserving insight into use, by summaries, clustering and minimum cluster sizes.
  Here: paraphrases with no names, 5 distinct members, summaries from shared sentences only.
- **CaMeL** (arXiv 2503.18813): separating the model that reads untrusted text from the code that acts. Here the
  summarizer and drafter have no tools, and code decides.
- **The Attacker Moves Second** (arXiv 2510.09023): adaptive attacks get past published defences. The guards are
  therefore one layer among several, and a guard that misses does not decide anything.
- **Community Notes** (Wojcik et al., arXiv 2210.15723): the matrix factorization whose intercept and factor decide
  whether a note bridges; the rule here (|factor| ≥ 0.5 waits) and the regularization are theirs.
- **Bridging Systems** (Ovadya and Thorburn, arXiv 2301.09976) and **Supernotes** (De et al., arXiv 2411.06116):
  ranking by support across groups rather than by volume.
- **Beyond Collusion Resistance** (Miller, Weyl and Erichsen, SSRN 4311507, 2022): connection-oriented cluster match,
  here the matching of each side's votes.
- **Collective Constitutional AI** (Huang et al., FAccT 2024): Polis' group-aware consensus, the other way to measure
  bridging that the review weighed.
- The term "loop engineering": Osmani, *Loop Engineering* (2026), and Lulla et al. (arXiv 2608.21884).

## How to run it

On the server, from `agent/`:
- `node --test test/loop_signals_unit.mjs test/loop_unit.mjs test/loop_ship_unit.mjs`: the loop's offline tests.
- `node test/loop_eval.mjs data/loop_eval.json`: the simulated run. It needs Muse Glimmer (`LLM_URL`; under
  `flock /tmp/serve-gpu.lock` on the 3090), Llama Guard 3 on `:11436`, Prompt Guard 2 and DRAMA on the CPU.
- `node test/loop_gates.mjs`: the gates.

In Messenger:
- `/improve on`;
- `/idea ask me about games in the evening`, then Share;
- `/ideas` (with its vote buttons);
- `/beta on`;
- `/forget`.

In the web app: the Club page's Ideas tab, and `#/maintain` with `MAINTAINER_TOKEN`. Add `?loop=live` to read the
gateway instead of the sample data.

The API: `GET /api/loop/ideas`, `POST /api/loop/vote`, `GET /api/loop/proposals`, `GET /api/loop/flags`, and with
the maintainer's token, `POST /api/loop/decide` and `POST /api/loop/rollback`.
