# Product context

## Register (product)

A product register: an app people use every week, not a brand or marketing page.

## Users, and where they are
- **Players on a phone**, between work and the next game: answering "want a game?", approving the week with a tap,
  logging what hurts, sharing a highlight. Phone width first, not a squeezed desktop.
- **The same players on a laptop or tablet**, planning the week, browsing the club and courts, or reading what their
  agent did and why. Dense information is welcome when the hierarchy makes it scannable.
- **A club's maintainer on a laptop**: reviewing the proposals the club asked for and the flags in a canary.
- **Everyone in Messenger**, where the same agent asks, reminds and checks in.

The job across all of them: take the organizing off the player, so a game gets played. A time that suits everyone, a
free court, the people they play best with, plans that bend around a sore knee or rain, and nothing booked without
their yes.

## Product purpose
Serve is a self-evolving sports social platform built against inertia: finding a court, finding people and matching
up is the hassle that stops people playing. Two ideas carry it:
- **The agent does the hassle.** Three helpers (Body, Match, Talk) watch for an open usual time, ask the player first,
  agree the game with the other players' agents, and plan around the body log and health app.
- **The community improves the platform.** Members' wishes become proposals that pass a safety review and ship behind
  flags to a few members first, turning themselves off when they do harm.

The club adds the social side: a feed, a leaderboard, challenges and social goals. Success is a game that gets played,
a player who says the organizing went away, and a club that sees its own wishes ship.

## Brand personality
**Calm, social, sure.**
- **Calm** where the agent works: plain sentences, the facts next to each step, quiet chrome.
- **Social** in the club: people's names and faces, the games they played, what the club asked for.
- **Sure** at the moment of decision: one clear question, one clear button, and the reason beside it.

The agent's voice is direct and warm, never chirpy, and it says what it did and what it did not.

## Anti-references (what it must not look like)
- Generic SaaS admin: rows of identical cards, KPI tiles everywhere, nothing that says sport.
- Gaming neon: glowing edges, cut-off corners, rainbow gradients.
- A research paper's figures: charts and tables pasted onto a page, so it reads as a lab demo rather than a product.
- Sports apps for children: mascots, primary colours everywhere, badges for everything.
- Fitness trackers that only count: steps and rings with no next game in sight.

## Design principles
1. **Every number has a source.** Figures sit next to where they come from (the calendar, the body log, the agent's
   answer); traceability is visible, not a footnote.
2. **One decision per screen.** A page opens on the single thing to act on, and keeps the detail a tap away for those
   who want it.
3. **Ask before acting.** The agent proposes and the player approves; nothing is booked without a tap, and the screen
   makes that plain.
4. **Show the work.** What the agent did, step by step, and which steps were the model's choice and which were code.
5. **Built for the hand it is in.** Phone-first questions and sharing, laptop density for planning: the layout changes
   with the device, not just its width.

## Accessibility and inclusion
- WCAG 2.2 AA: body text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, including placeholders and muted text, in light and dark.
- Touch targets ≥ 44 px; the main question and its buttons readable at arm's length.
- Colour is never the only signal; pair it with labels or position.
- Reduced motion respected everywhere; highlight and map animations get a calm alternative.
- The interface is in English.
